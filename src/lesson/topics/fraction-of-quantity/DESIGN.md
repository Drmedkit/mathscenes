# Fraction of a quantity (grade 5, 5.NF.B.4a) — design

**Concept.** a/b of q is a copies of one of b equal shares of q: (q ÷ b) × a = a/b × q. The bottom number cuts the
whole into equal shares, the top number counts how many shares you take. Taking more shares than make the whole
gives more than the whole; a share does not have to be a whole number (1/4 of 6 ft = 6/4 ft).

**Model: `FractionOf`** (concreteness fading inside one block). A set of painted objects (cookies / marbles) slides
into b teal trays, and under it the SAME quantity is a tape cut into b sections lined up under the trays, each
labelled with the share (lavender). The a taken trays and sections light up lime; a brace under them carries the
answer. a > b: the whole keeps its brace and the extra sections are lighter copies past it. `ticks` shows unit
cells for fractional shares. `scale` draws two tapes to one scale (the proof). Steps: labelQ, objects, group,
labelB, tape, total, cut, share, take, count. Colour roles: amber = the whole, teal = the equal groups, lavender =
one share, lime = the answer, red = wrong. Notation sits on the board at the height of the part it names; leaders
go only to the nearest part (the rightmost tray / section label, the answer under the tape); the group labels pulse.

## The five parts
1. **The question** — 12 cookies, Maya eats 3/4. Cards 9 / 4 (12 ÷ 3: divides by the top) / 3 (stops at one share) + how sure.
2. **Worked example** — Name the whole (label pulses, the tape of the same 12 appears) → Make 4 equal groups (cookies
   into trays, tape cut, shares 3; `12 ÷ 4 = 3`, leader to the nearest tray) → Take 3 of the groups (trays + sections
   lime, brace "9 cookies"; `3 × 3 = 9`, leader to the answer) → Write it (`3/4 of 12 = 9`, `(12 ÷ 4) × 3 = 9`).
   Why: "why divide by 4, not 3?" — right / groups of 4 (misgrouped) / 3 means 3 cookies (numerator as count).
3. **Your turn** — 2/3 of a 15 ft ribbon, tape only (fading: objects gone). Given: whole, cut. Kid: one part (5),
   two parts (10), the expression (choice), then 4/3 of the ribbon: more or less than 15 ft? → a 4th section past the
   whole, 20 ft. Callback to the part-1 pick.
4. **Prove it** — Riley: 3/4 of 28 ft = 18 ft. Chain: one share would be 6 ft (18 ÷ 3) → the whole would be 24 ft, not
   28 (a second tape at the same scale grows to 24, the 4 ft gap is outlined red) → so it is 21 ft (28 ÷ 4 × 3).
   Distractor chips: 18 ÷ 4 (part divided by denominator), 6 × 3 = 18 (rebuilds with numerator), "the sums are right".
5. **Practice** — runtime walk through all 15 sub-goals, then mixed.
